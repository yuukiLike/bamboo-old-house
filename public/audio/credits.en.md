# A Home Among the Bamboo · Sound credits

The soundscape uses outdoor field recordings. Thank you to the recordists who made these sounds available to share.

These recordings were **not made at my grandmother’s house**. They are arranged to accompany this remembered landscape. The dawn birds were recorded in Staffordshire, England; the cicadas in Ausonia, Italy. The bamboo and night-insect source pages do not specify a recording location. The frog was recorded beside a small pond at night.

The Freesound recordings are released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); Joseph SARDIN’s recordings use [BigSoundBank’s CC0 / public-domain terms](https://bigsoundbank.com/licenses.html). The recordists and hosting sites do not endorse this project.

The light-rain and leaf-rain files came from Rain Sounds for Sleeping’s public MP3 mirrors, cross-checked against its [itemized source list](https://rainsoundsforsleeping.com/licensing/). The other rain and frog files came from the recordist’s website. The sound placed beside the eaves is a recording of rain on concrete, not a recording of a roof.

The original wind, birds, cicadas and crickets use excerpts from public high-quality MP3 files, cut without re-encoding. Rain and the short frog call were trimmed, gently filtered, levelled and re-encoded as high-quality VBR MP3. They are not described as lossless masters. Original stereo is retained where available; the ground drops and frog are mono.

Loops use six-second crossfades. Their balance changes with the time, weather and viewpoint; indoor listening is softened. The frog is a short, occasional call rather than a loop. Heavy rain leaves only wind and rain. The leaf sound follows the same gusts as the visible bamboo. There is no added music or synthesized wildlife.

Audio loads only after you turn on sound or choose a listening scene. Rain and frog recordings load when needed. Playback fades and pauses when the tab is hidden. All playback files are hosted by this site; listening does not contact Freesound.
